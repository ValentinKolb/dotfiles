# Argument, evidence, and paper review

Use the seminar-paper path by default. Add the publication extension only when
the document is an actual research paper or the user explicitly requests it.

## Seminar papers and academic coursework

### Research question and scope

Check whether the research question is specific enough to answer within the
document and whether the body actually answers it. Distinguish the topic from
the question and the question from the answer or thesis.

Flag sections that are interesting but do not advance the stated purpose. Do
not demand an explicit thesis when the discipline or assignment expects a
different form of inquiry.

### Argument map

Reconstruct the shortest honest map of the reasoning:

```text
Research question
  -> necessary background or definitions
  -> evidence or analysis
  -> intermediate conclusions
  -> answer and limitations
```

For each section and paragraph, identify its contribution to this map. Look for
missing premises, circular reasoning, contradictions, false dichotomies,
unsupported generalization, and conclusions that exceed the evidence.

Treat counterarguments as useful when they test a material premise or
interpretation. Do not add ritual “both sides” discussion when no credible
alternative matters to the question.

### Source use

A citation near a sentence does not prove that the source supports the claim.
Where the source is available, verify the exact proposition and context. Where
it is not available, phrase the finding as a question or verification need.

Check whether the text:

- distinguishes paraphrase, quotation, reported evidence, and the author's own
  analysis;
- cites claims that are neither common knowledge nor established by the text;
- represents the source's population, conditions, uncertainty, and conclusion
  fairly;
- relies on a secondary account when the primary source is needed;
- uses quotations for a reason rather than as a substitute for explanation;
- synthesizes sources instead of presenting an annotated list of them;
- keeps bibliographic and in-text references internally consistent.

Never manufacture a citation, locator, quotation, DOI, page number, or reference
entry. If a claim lacks support, mark it unresolved.

### Structure

Evaluate structure by function, not by a universal template. A seminar paper
often needs:

- an introduction that establishes the problem, question, scope, approach, and
  relevance without previewing empty ceremony;
- a body whose sections follow the logic of the inquiry;
- explicit transitions where the logical relationship is not otherwise clear;
- a conclusion that answers the question, states limits, and does not introduce
  unsupported new claims.

Recommend reordering before rewriting transitions when the underlying sequence
is wrong.

## Publication extension

For an empirical paper, reconcile:

- research question, design, method, and claimed inference;
- planned versus exploratory or post hoc analysis;
- population, sample, exclusions, time points, variables, and units;
- methods with every reported result;
- numbers and labels across abstract, prose, tables, and figures;
- estimates with uncertainty and appropriate denominators;
- limitations with the actual threats to inference and generalizability.

Do not convert association into causation or non-significance into equivalence.
Preserve negative, null, adverse, unexpected, failed, and inconclusive findings
when they belong to the study record.

Use a reporting guideline only when it matches the actual study and article
type. Consult the current official guideline and target venue rather than
relying on remembered requirements. A checklist supports review; it does not
certify scientific quality or acceptance.

Authorship, contributions, conflicts, funding, ethics, data availability, AI
disclosures, and submission approval require verified human decisions. Do not
complete them from plausible boilerplate.

## Confidentiality and tools

Treat unpublished manuscripts, reviewer comments, restricted sources,
participant data, and proprietary material according to their actual policy
and authorization boundary. Prefer local processing when external transfer is
not clearly authorized. Removing names alone is not necessarily sufficient
de-identification.

The optional Harper CLI is local and suitable only for an English grammar pass.
It does not verify evidence, argumentation, citations, scientific consistency,
or German prose.

## Prioritization

Rank findings by their effect:

1. misleading, unsupported, or contradictory claims;
2. reasoning gaps that prevent the research question from being answered;
3. structural problems that obscure the argument;
4. recurring prose problems that impair comprehension;
5. local style and grammar issues.

Do not let a long list of minor edits hide a substantive defect.
